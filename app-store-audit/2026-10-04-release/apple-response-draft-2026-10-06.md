# How to Adult — Apple response preparation

Apple submission `69e6815f-801c-49b8-bb0f-41be9dd97694`, iOS 1.0 (1), rejected October 5 under 4.3 and 4.2.6. This document is an unsent draft about the submitted pocket-guide build, not the unshipped Assistant.

1. **Function and problem.** The submitted app contains 56 practical guides across seven everyday topics, with 224 individually checkable steps. Readers search locally, inspect supplies/cautions/source links, save guides, retain progress and schedule local reminders. A public catalog can update the library, while a bundled and last-valid copy supports offline use. Personal search, bookmarks, progress and reminders remain local; catalog hosting retains disclosed connection diagnostics. It addresses people needing concrete steps during an unfamiliar practical task.

2. **Audience.** The intended audience is people learning everyday independent-living tasks, including laundry, simple cooking, home upkeep, work administration and friendships. It includes readers who need a short, usable procedure rather than a long article. United States-specific financial and administrative guides visibly identify their scope. This is the documented product intention; no measured user demographics are claimed.

3. **Specific contribution.** One local searchable library combines task supplies, cautions, source links, checkable progress, saved guides and dated reminders across multiple life-skill topics. The current record does not establish that competing products cannot do the same. No market-exclusivity claim should be sent. A substantive owner explanation or substantial differentiated successor is needed for this question.

4. **Beta testing.** Documented engineering verification includes real Simulator notification delivery and opening the intended guide, denial/recovery, cancellation, dark appearance and larger text, persistence and signed-package checks. These are engineering checks, not independent-user beta feedback. No external beta tester feedback is documented. Owner fact required: whether real users tested the app, what they reported and which resulting changes shipped. If none, answer that plainly.

5. **Standalone or suite.** This is an independent life-skills guide. Its selected release is separate from the portfolio's focused recipe, calendar, contact, notes, résumé and game products. This statement describes the technical product boundaries, not an invented commercial suite. Owner should confirm the business relationship.

6. **Consolidation.** The dedicated seven-topic guide library, task-level progress and local guide reminders form a coherent independent workflow. The record does not establish that integration into another app is impossible. The owner must confirm why it is maintained separately; avoid claiming technical necessity.

7. **Shared portfolio components.** The frozen native project uses SwiftUI and Apple system frameworks without third-party runtime package dependencies. The current pocket-guide implementation has its own AdultStore, models, views, catalog and icon assets. This repository alone cannot prove whether original code/assets were reused in another portfolio app. Owner history confirmation remains required before saying none.

8. **Third-party sharing.** The frozen project does not declare a third-party runtime SDK or content-library dependency. Source links point to original authoritative webpages rather than bundling their full articles. Templates, historical source origin and ownership outside this repository still require owner confirmation.

9. **Client/content provider.** The app is under Nathan Fennel's developer account, with original app identity preserved. Whether a client, partner or third party supplied its concept, branding or content cannot be established from the repository. Owner fact required. Do not infer ownership solely from copyright strings.
